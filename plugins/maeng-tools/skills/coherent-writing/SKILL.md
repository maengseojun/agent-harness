# Coherent writing

Write a piece the reader can follow from one idea to the next, while retaining the information that determines its meaning. Coherence comes from the reasoning, not from sprinkling transition words over unrelated facts. Apply this skill during composition, not only during proofreading.

## Before drafting

Infer the reader, purpose, genre, length and voice from the request. Ask only if a missing choice materially changes the piece. For an explanation, identify the question to answer; for persuasion, identify the claim to defend; for a narrative, identify the experience or change to follow. Do not convert every piece into an argumentative essay.

Use a compact private working map, proportional to the task:

- **Through-line:** the one sentence the whole piece needs the reader to understand. Let the evidence change this sentence.
- **Material:** for each consequential fact, retain its source, population, time, units, denominator, conditions, uncertainty and intended role. Do not fill missing denominators or measurement methods from plausible context. Separate observed fact, source interpretation, your inference, recommendation and hypothetical illustration.
- **Coverage:** mark required information and inconvenient evidence. Group by the reader's questions, not by the order of sources. Omit material that does not serve the purpose; do not omit facts that change the conclusion.

For source-heavy work, make this map explicit in working notes so factual compression can be checked. A short supplied draft may need only an internal pass. Use the task's research workflow if a claim needs verification; this skill alone does not require browsing every writing request.

## Build the reasoning before polishing

Arrange paragraph jobs in the order a reader needs them. Useful movements include problem → explanation → consequence, question → evidence → qualified answer, or scene → interpretation → wider significance. Choose the movement that fits; do not impose a five-paragraph template or always lead with a thesis when the requested genre needs a scene.

For each paragraph, decide what it contributes to the through-line and what question it leaves for the next paragraph. If the next paragraph does not answer or develop that question, move it, supply the missing reasoning, or remove it.

Develop substantive claims with **evidence and interpretation**. A number or quotation does not explain itself: state why it bears on the claim and how far the inference reaches. Use an example when it makes an abstract relation concrete. Label invented examples; a vivid anecdote is not prevalence or causal evidence.

For a contested conclusion or proposal, consider the strongest relevant alternative reading, drawback or objection. Represent it fairly and respond with evidence, a concession, a narrower claim, or a revised conclusion. Do not invent an opponent or force a ceremonial rebuttal into a simple explanation. Unresolved contrary evidence belongs in the piece when it changes what the reader should believe or do.

## Draft connected prose

- Give a paragraph one dominant job. Its central sentence should say what the evidence means, rather than merely announce a topic. Place it where the reader can use it; not every paragraph must start the same way.
- Link new information to something the reader already knows. Reuse a stable key term or refer clearly to the previous finding. Write the actual relationship: cause, contrast, qualification, example, or consequence. “Therefore” cannot supply a missing causal step.
- Keep consequential conditions beside their claims. Do not let a confident opening contradict a caveat buried at the end.
- Explain a concept before depending on it. Avoid forward references that make the reader hold unexplained terms in memory.
- End a paragraph with the implication earned by its evidence or a useful opening for the next idea. Avoid repeating its topic sentence as a miniature conclusion. End the piece with the answer or consequence it has earned, without adding unsupported claims.
- Use lists and headings for genuinely parallel items or navigation. Keep relationships and reasoning in prose when a list would hide them.

## Edit in the target language

**Korean:** use natural Korean word order, clear topic continuity and concrete verbs. Reduce stacked noun phrases and empty expressions such as “관련하여” or “측면에서” when they hide the action. An omitted subject is fine when its referent is clear; restore it when actors change. Keep endings appropriate and consistent, and vary rhythm without using fragments to fake emphasis. Do not mechanically import English subject–verb proximity into Korean, or delete qualifications just to shorten sentences.

Use Korean commas sparingly. Do not carry English comma rhythms into Korean or insert a comma at every clause boundary. Prefer natural connective endings or separate sentences when they make the relationship clearer; retain commas needed for enumeration or ambiguity. Do not impose a comma ban or count-based quality score.

**English:** keep the actor and action easy to find; move long interruptions when they bury the predicate. Use familiar information to establish context and place the intended new emphasis where the sentence can support it. Prefer concrete verbs to heavy nominalizations, but preserve useful passive voice when the object or process is the focus. Vary sentence length for reasoning and emphasis, not to satisfy a word-count formula.

**Both:** preserve terminology, numerical meaning, uncertainty and authorial voice. Translate the argument's function into natural prose rather than copying the other language's syntax. Avoid slogans, ornamental metaphors, generic importance claims and repeated “not X but Y” constructions unless they clarify a real distinction.

Keep references easy to resolve while reading. If “this”, “that”, “the former” or an equivalent points across several sentences or competing subjects, repeat the short concrete term or bring its explanation closer. Reduce chains of backward references and source-link detours without removing necessary attribution. Explain the relevant evidence in the prose so the reader need not open a link or return to an earlier section to follow the argument.

## Review the actual draft

Quality layer: for a non-code result the user will show or submit, follow `maeng-harness:quality-layer` when the maeng-harness plugin is enabled (criteria from real examples, independent check, closing walkthrough).

For work that will become a report, deck or diagram, review the connected explanation before dividing it into visual units. Keep the claim, its support and the interpretation together; a neat arrangement cannot repair a missing reasoning step. Reuse accepted reader and interview decisions rather than asking about sentence order or conclusion placement again.

Apply optional expression-editing guidance such as Humanizer only after the reasoning pass, when concrete excess remains. Compare the edit against the material map for changed facts, conditions and uncertainty. Do not count added facts as a style improvement, mandate a second editor on every draft, or treat an agent score as reader acceptance.

Read a reverse outline: summarize each paragraph's contribution in a sentence. Do those sentences develop the through-line, or merely list topics? Repair gaps, repetitions and unsupported leaps before surface editing.

Then compare the draft with the material map:

1. Are required facts and conclusion-changing conditions retained with the right source and scope?
2. Does each important claim have adequate support and an explanation of the inference? Are uncertainty and disagreement visible where needed?
3. Are examples distinguishable from evidence? Have correlation, self-report or a small sample become a causal or universal claim?
4. Can the reader explain why each paragraph follows the previous one? Can references be resolved nearby without repeatedly looking back or opening links? In Korean, are commas needed for meaning rather than inherited from English sentence rhythm?
5. Does the piece meet the requested genre, language, length and voice without redundant conclusions or needless scaffolding?

Deliver the finished prose by default. A one-sentence rewrite normally needs only a direct edit and a meaning check. Show the reasoning map or revision notes only when requested or when an unresolved limitation matters. Never present invented support to make a piece read smoothly.
