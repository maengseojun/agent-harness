# Pitch preparation

Establish audience, desired next action, speaking time, product stage, and any existing deck or brand constraints. Ask only for missing details that materially alter the pitch; preserve decisions in the brief.

Find an opening question, contrast or concrete scene that matters to this
audience. Test whether it leads naturally to the product claim rather than
serving as decoration. Put the spoken explanation in notes when it would crowd
the slide; select imagery and layout that make the intended point immediately
visible at presentation size.

Build a narrative appropriate to the audience: problem and affected user, why existing approaches fall short, solution and demonstration, supporting evidence, relevant business or operational implications, and the ask. This is a reasoning guide, not a mandatory slide sequence.

Distinguish working capabilities, prototype behavior, plans, and hypotheses. Trace traction, market, performance, and financial claims to supporting evidence; label estimates and assumptions. Never promote a planned capability into a demonstrated result.

Use slide titles that convey the point. Keep explanatory depth in speaker notes when requested or useful. Match the amount of content to speaking time. Prepare Q&A around the weakest assumptions, alternatives, evidence gaps, implementation feasibility, and the audience's likely objections; answers must preserve uncertainty.

Verify consistency across the deck, notes, demo, and Q&A. Inspect the rendered slides for clipping, legibility, chart labels, and source notes using the presentation skill. A polished deck does not establish that its claims are true.
