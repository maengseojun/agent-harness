# Prepare a Work deliverable

Use the supplied brief and accepted decisions. If absent, infer a compact working brief and clarify only consequential gaps. Match the requested language and adapt prose to the deliverable. Work is the user's default destination for these tasks; do not transfer the task automatically or refuse work in the current surface when the user chose it.

Apply [work-records — Project/task workflow](../work-records/SKILL.md#projecttask-workflow)
before execution/resumption: read task → Spec/Plan → Architecture/Rules → dependencies.
Use its criteria, lead/handoff, change and offline rules for this deliverable;
an inferred brief does not approve draft criteria. Keep evidence current, leave
Linear Done for user confirmation and avoid Architecture overhead for standalone tasks.

Interview the user only about choices they own that cannot be inferred from the
materials and would change the result: audience and desired response, central
claim, required evidence, venue or format rules, and a consequential style
preference. Decide routine structure, wording and layout from the brief and
references. Do not turn every design choice into a question.

## Evidence and context

Select available source connectors and artifact skills relevant to this output. Read the actual primary material behind important claims. Use computer interaction when required by the source or visual task. Keep a compact source record for substantial research: claim, source location, relevant support, date when material, and uncertainty or conflicting evidence. Separate observations, estimates, and recommendations. Do not treat a search snippet as verified source content.

Fetch only relevant pages, sections, or ranges. Reuse gathered evidence across the report, deck, and speaker notes. If a required source is inaccessible, identify the resulting gap; do not silently replace it with a confident claim.

## Argument and prose

Before drafting, establish the main conclusion or central question and the role each section plays. Organize around the reader's reasoning needs rather than one summary per source. Explain why evidence supports a conclusion and address material counterevidence. Avoid causal claims when sources establish only correlation.

For a report or paper, map the argument from claim to evidence and from one
section to the next before formatting; follow the actual assignment, journal or
supplied Word template when one exists. For a presentation, draft the opening
hook and a one-sentence takeaway per slide, then choose what to show, what to
say and what belongs in notes. Review the sequence as a talk, not as isolated
pages. Present an outline or representative page for user input when a remaining
editorial choice would materially change the artifact.

For reports, develop connected paragraphs with concrete explanations and examples where helpful. For decision documents, connect findings to implications and actionable choices. Do not add owners, budgets, or recommendations without a basis. For pitches, read [pitch guidance](references/pitch.md).

Review the draft for unsupported leaps, missing mechanisms, repetition, abrupt transitions, and unnecessary headings. Remove generic introductory and concluding filler. Preserve useful detail rather than equating quality with brevity. Do not invent data to make the narrative persuasive.

## Production and verification

For design-sensitive slides or documents, inspect any supplied reference and
extract its reusable visual rules before authoring. When the direction remains
unclear, show a few representative layouts with real content and choose a
direction before scaling production. Keep native text and simple diagrams
editable when the requested format supports it.

Use the available document, presentation, spreadsheet, or PDF skill when producing those formats; follow its actual artifact and rendering requirements. Inspect the rendered output when layout matters. Verify material figures and citations against the source record. Distinguish file existence, layout verification, and substantive verification.

Deliver the artifact with a concise account of verification and material limitations. Stop when the brief is satisfied. Do not impose multiple rewrite rounds unless a concrete defect remains.
