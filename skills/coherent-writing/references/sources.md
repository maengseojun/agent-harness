# Source-informed methods

Checked 2026-10-08. These are selected instructional sources and a readable model, not proof of a universally best style or a measured improvement in this skill. Summaries below are original paraphrases. The workflow combines these ideas with editorial judgment; not every instruction is a quotation or research finding.

## S. Keshav — How to Read a Paper (2007)

[ACM SIGCOMM CCR original PDF](http://ccr.sigcomm.org/online/files/p83-keshavA.pdf), volume 37, number 3, pp. 83–84. Access: browser opened the original PDF; both pages were extracted and read locally. This is the 2007 article, not a later revised version.

The piece introduces a practical problem, gives the method's overall shape, and develops its stages with different goals and stopping choices. A reader can understand the approach before meeting its details. Section 2.1 also advises writers to make section titles coherent and abstracts concise and comprehensive for a first reading.

**Apply:** give the reader an orientation before details; make the organization recoverable on a quick reading; scale detail to the reader's purpose. The skill's reverse-outline check is an adaptation, not Keshav's own writing algorithm. This is primarily a reading-method article. Do not repeat all its domain-specific advice as universal evidence standards or claim its three passes establish a mandatory writing process.

## Harvard College Writing Center — Anatomy of a Body Paragraph

[Official guide](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph). Access: full main guide text read through Aside; illustrative image was not visually evaluated.

**Apply:** make a paragraph's main idea serve the larger argument; supply evidence and explain the inference it supports. Paragraph count follows the reasoning needed. A useful ending adds an earned understanding instead of repeating the paragraph or introducing an unsupported point. The guide explicitly allows variation in paragraph structure.

**Boundary:** strongest fit is academic argument. A narrative scene, transition or brief workplace message need not contain three visibly separate components.

## Harvard College Writing Center — Counterargument

[Official guide](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/counterargument). Access: full main guide text read through Aside; example image was not visually evaluated.

**Apply:** anticipate reasonable objections while developing an argument; test assumptions, alternative interpretations and neglected consequences. Explain the response or concede and narrow the thesis. An objection that survives scrutiny can require changing the conclusion. Put the objection where its answer matters, rather than attaching it mechanically at the end.

**Boundary:** consider relevant objections, not every imaginable one. Do not manufacture disagreement or equate weak assertions with strong evidence.

## 유시민 interview — “당신은 왜 글을 쓰는가?” (2016-07-06)

[Original interview, YES24 채널예스, 엄지혜](https://m.ch.yes24.com/article/details/31142). Access: full interview body read through Aside. This is an interview containing the writer's answers, not a column authored by him or a reading of his entire book.

**Apply:** form a question before collecting information; ask why a belief holds and why it might not; judge whether sentences express the intended meaning accurately and reasoning holds together. The interview develops the question-led research advice through a concrete search example, making an abstract instruction easier to understand.

**Boundary:** these are the interviewee's writing judgments, not controlled evidence of effectiveness. Do not import his political positions, copy his personal voice or turn context-specific advice about emotion into a universal percentage rule. This source supplies a Korean writer's method, not a claim that the public has ranked the article's prose quality.

## George D. Gopen & Judith A. Swan — The Science of Scientific Writing (1990)

[TU/e selected quotations, compiled by Tom Verhoeff](https://wstomv.win.tue.nl/quotes/science-of-scientific-writing.html). [Original bibliographic entry](https://www.jstor.org/stable/29774235), American Scientist 78(6), pp. 550–558.

Access: the TU/e excerpt page was read. The full original was not verified; JSTOR initially exposed a loading page. Treat this as a supplementary excerpt source, not a full-paper review.

**Apply:** connect familiar information to new material; place context and emphasis deliberately; express actions clearly. The excerpts explicitly reject a fixed algorithm for good writing. These ideas explain why inserting “moreover” into disconnected sentences is insufficient.

**Boundary:** sentence-position guidance was developed for English scientific prose. Korean adaptations in SKILL.md are editorial choices, not claims experimentally established by this source. Subject–verb adjacency must not be imposed on Korean syntax.
